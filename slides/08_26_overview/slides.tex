\begin{frame}{Why study discrete math \emph{now}?}

\begin{alertblock}{Let's grant the strongest assumption}
\small
Assume AI writes correct code --- and even research-level proofs --- better and faster than you.
\textbf{Don't argue with it. Just assume it.} The interesting question is what's left over.
\end{alertblock}

\pause
\vfill
We say the goal is \textbf{``solve the problem.''} That was never actually the goal:

\vfill

\small Only the \textbf{first} stage was ever an explicit goal --- and it is exactly the stage being automated.
Every stage downstream of it is an act of \textbf{reasoning about correctness}. That is this course.

\begin{frame}{What that buys you: studies, life, work}

\begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
\begin{column}{0.31\textwidth}
{\centering\textbf{\alert{Your studies}}\par}
\vspace{0.4em}
{\scriptsize\raggedright
An AI can hand you correct homework. It cannot hand you the \emph{ability} that the homework existed to build.

\vspace{0.4em}
Induction, invariants, counting, graphs: these are the tools that let you say \textbf{``is right''} instead of \textbf{``looks right.''}\par}
\end{column}
\pause
\begin{column}{0.31\textwidth}
{\centering\textbf{\alert{Your life}}\par}
\vspace{0.4em}
{\scriptsize\raggedright
Generative models are \emph{ungrounded}: they optimize for output that \emph{appears} satisfactory, not for the property it is supposed to indicate.

\vspace{0.4em}
So you need a check that does not depend on the answer looking good. \textbf{One counterexample settles it.}\par}
\end{column}
\pause
\begin{column}{0.31\textwidth}
{\centering\textbf{\alert{Your job}}\par}
\vspace{0.4em}
{\scriptsize\raggedright
Code is going from \textbf{scarce} to \textbf{abundant}. The bottleneck (and the salary) moves downstream.

\vspace{0.4em}

\begin{frame}{Virtues of developing mathematical reasoning}

\begin{evidence}[blue]
\textbf{It transfers to \emph{other} subjects.} Over one year, students taking post-compulsory mathematics improved at \textbf{conditional reasoning} more than matched literature students,   despite having \emph{no instruction in logic at all}.
\evsrc{Attridge \& Inglis, \textit{PLoS ONE} 8(7), 2013.}
\end{evidence}

\pause
\begin{evidence}[orange]
\textbf{It is exactly what AI does \emph{not} hand you.} Given an AI assistant, developers wrote \alert{less secure} code, but were \alert{more likely to believe it was secure}. Those who
\textbf{trusted the AI least and interrogated it most} wrote the fewest vulnerabilities.
\evsrc{Perry, Srivastava, Kumar \& Boneh, \textit{ACM CCS}, 2023.}
\end{evidence}

\pause
\begin{evidence}[green]
\textbf{It pays.} For the marginal student, \textbf{one extra high-school math course $\Rightarrow$ $\sim$10\% higher earnings} via a shift into more cognitively skilled work.
\evsrc{Goodman, \textit{J.\ Labor Economics} 37(4), 2019. \quad Joensen \& Nielsen, \textit{JHR} 44(1), 2009: 20--25\% per advanced course.}
\end{evidence}
